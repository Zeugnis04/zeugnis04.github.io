## Introduction to Japanese pitch accent

Most Japanese dialects have a feature called *pitch accent* --- where words are distinguished by tones that fall on specific *mora*{% sidenote '' '[[Mora (Wikipedia)]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mora_(linguistics)) A mora is a smallest unit of timing, equal to or shorter than a syllable, that theoretically or perceptually exists in some spoken languages in which phonetic length (such as vowel length) matters significantly. In Japanese, consonants like <span lang="ja">ん</span> are also monomoraic.'%}.

For instance, let's take a look at the Tokyo dialect. <span lang="ja"><ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby></span> and <span lang="ja"><ruby>飴<rt>あめ</rt></ruby></span> has different pitch patterns. The former starts high and dips at the next mora, and vice versa the latter. From now on, we will place a red bar over morae with high tone as such: 雨 <span class="pitch-high">あ</span>め and 飴 あ<span class="pitch-high">め</span>.

Pitch patterns, however, vary by dialects as shown below.

|          | 東京         | 高知         | 鹿児島       |
|----------|:--------------|:--------------|:--------------|
| 飴、飴が | あ<span class="pitch-high">め</span>、あ<span class="pitch-high">めが</span> | <span class="pitch-high">あめ</span>、<span class="pitch-high">あめが</span> | <span class="pitch-high">あ</span>め、あ<span class="pitch-high">め</span>が |
| 山、山が | や<span class="pitch-high">ま</span>、や<span class="pitch-high">ま</span>が | <span class="pitch-high">や</span>ま、<span class="pitch-high">や</span>まが | や<span class="pitch-high">ま</span>、やま<span class="pitch-high">が</span> |
| 雨、雨が | <span class="pitch-high">あ</span>め、<span class="pitch-high">あ</span>めが | あ<span class="pitch-high">め</span>、あ<span class="pitch-high">め</span>が | あ<span class="pitch-high">め</span>、あめ<span class="pitch-high">が</span> |

It is easy to realize that the three cases have different accent patterns for Tokyo and Kōchi. At Kagoshima, 山 and 雨 seem to have identical accent patterns. For now, it is enough to realize that pitch accents actually do vary between various Japanese dialects.
