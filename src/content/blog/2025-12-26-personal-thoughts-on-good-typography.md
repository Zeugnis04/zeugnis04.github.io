## Introduction

{% newthought "What is good typography?" %} Typography, despite being an essential part of our daily life, often gets overlooked or underappreciated by most people. That is understandable: only a small circle of professional designers or deeply online people keeps up with typography discussions, and the rest of us simply consume whatever is printed or rendered in front of us. My own interest started in a small, almost mundane way. Nobody forced me into the ideology of typography supremacy or anything. I just found myself wondering why certain fonts were used where they were. The earliest memory I have is the script for a Christmas play I did as a kindergartener{% sidenote 'hilariously' 'hilariously, which was using Comic Sans'%}. I remember watching the Microsoft Word document to see which font was chosen and then rushing home to try it on our family computer.

That small curiosity turned into an interest in fonts, typography, better typesetting, and so on, and I have become an amateur typography nerd. Consider this post a quick set of pointers: things in typography that feel intriguing to me and maybe worth looking into if the topic still feels distant. I am nowhere near a professional nor an expert, so please take my views as a grain of salt. There are much better people in the industry who have sharper ideas than me, but hopefully this gives a bit of context on what I find fun about typography.

## Typography is everywhere

{% newthought "Typography is everywhere."%} Since most of us reading this blog live in a text society, literally everything around us contains text. Some text comes from signs vying for our attention, some is tiny print spelling out disadvantageous conditions just to satisfy regulations, and some is plain text written to deliver information. Whatever form it takes, the text we see is either (a) handwriting, (b) a printed version of some typeface, or (c) a rendered version{%sidenote 'screen' 'as in a screen or your computer monitor'%} of some typeface. While (a) is one of my interests, (b) and (c) are what we usually think of as *typography*. In other words, *typography* is simply too important. We see typefaces everywhere, yet rarely appreciate or even notice the quality of the typefaces that surround us.

I am not sure if this is intriguing enough, but I still hope more people find themselves curious about typography this way.
