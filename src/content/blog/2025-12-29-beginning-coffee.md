I wanted to try brewing my own coffee for a long time, and finally here they are. My coffee experience was usually limited to spontaneous experiences in specialty coffee when I had the chance, and I thought that I can start brewing them myself.

One of my colleagues owned an Aeropress, so I decided to start with it. I was mostly inspired by [James Hoffmann's ultimate Aeropress technique](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j6VlT_jUVPc), and was pleased with the result, so sticked to it for some time.

{% maincolumn '/assets/img/beginning-coffee/aeroopress.webp' 'Aeropress' %}

It felt approachable, less intimidating than a V60 setup, and compact enough that I could keep it out on the counter without it looking like a science experiment. But thanks to a friend, who gave me a set of V60 server and dripper, I decided to try pour over recipes too.

{% maincolumn '/assets/img/beginning-coffee/dripgeisha.webp' 'V60 and Panama Geisha Janson #501 Washed' %}

And of course I had to go bougie with beans immediately. The iconic jasmine note is just unbelievable. This was from Seoul CafeShow 2025, from New Wave Coffee Roasters.

{% maincolumn '/assets/img/beginning-coffee/newcup.webp' 'New cup for the ritual' %}

And a new cup from SNU Pottery Sales. SNU College of Fine Arts sells ceramic products made by students every year, by the end of the year. This gorgeous cup is from the event.

{% maincolumn '/assets/img/beginning-coffee/clarimentogeisha.webp' 'Clarimento Geisha pourover' %}

I have also been visiting many different places that serve specialty coffee. This is from Lounge Clarimento in Hapjeong. With a friend, we shared Costa Rica La Esmeralda SL28 & Geisha Honey and Costa Rica Hacienda Copey Takara #200 Geisha Anaerobic Washed.

I will share more updates on the explorations I make!
